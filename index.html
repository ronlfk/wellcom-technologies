The technology

The hard part isn't shipping. It's season four.

Every system below started as something we needed for our own games, broke in production, and rebuilt properly.

Rollback netcode

Deterministic simulation with client-side rollback, so a high-latency connection feels like a local match. Shipped across every title we run.

Matchmaking that waits less

Skill, latency and party size balanced in one pass. We publish our real queue times at peak and off-peak, and we hold ourselves to them.

Zero-downtime seasons

New seasons roll out behind flags while matches are in progress — no maintenance window, no scheduled downtime, no "back at 6am".

Live-ops console

Designers tune drop rates, events and pricing without a build. Every change is versioned, reviewed and reversible in one click.

Anti-cheat and trust

Server-authoritative state plus behavioural detection. Bans are reviewed by humans, and every ban can be appealed to one.

One account, every device

Cross-progression and cross-play across PC, console and mobile, with platform entitlements reconciled in under a second.

By the numbers

A studio that has shipped, and kept shipping.

1

Small teams, whole features

Six to nine people own a feature end to end — design, client, server, analytics. No hand-off to a separate "live team" after launch.

2

Playable every Friday

Everything builds and is playable internally at the end of every week, from the first prototype. Ideas that only work in a document get found out early.

3

Players in the room

A rotating group of community members plays unreleased seasons under NDA and sits in the retro afterwards. They have killed features. Good.

4

No crunch as a plan

Scope gets cut before hours get added. We would rather move a launch than ask a team to work a weekend.
