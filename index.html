How we build

The hard part of a simple game.

Casual looks effortless from the outside, which is the whole point. Underneath, these are the systems that make it possible.

Level tooling for designers

Designers build, test and publish levels themselves, from a browser, without waiting on a build. The content pipeline is the product.

Difficulty that reads the room

A level that stops being fun is a level someone quits on. We tune pacing from real play data, per cohort, rather than guessing at a global curve.

Experiments, not opinions

Every change that touches the player ships behind a flag and gets measured. Arguments about a button colour end with a number, not a meeting.

Offline first

Trains, lifts and bad hotel wifi are where casual games actually get played. Everything works offline and reconciles when the signal comes back.

One save, every device

Start a level on a phone, finish it on a tablet. Progress follows the player, and nothing is lost when they change handset.

Playable by more people

Colour-blind safe palettes, one-handed layouts, scalable text and no puzzle that depends on reaction speed. Casual should mean casual.

The studio

How we work.

1

Small teams, whole features

A handful of people own a feature end to end — design, client, server, analytics. No hand-off to a separate live team after launch.

2

Playable every Friday

Everything builds and is playable internally at the end of every week, from the first prototype. Ideas that only work on paper get found out early.

3

Players in the room

We put unreleased builds in front of real players and sit in the retro afterwards. They have killed features of ours. Good.

4

No crunch as a plan

Scope gets cut before hours get added. We would rather move a launch than ask a team to work a weekend.
